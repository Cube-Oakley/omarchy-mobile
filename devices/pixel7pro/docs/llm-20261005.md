# A small language model on the phone (October 5, 2026)

The first step of `docs/ai-control-plan-20261005.md`: how fast a small
instruction model runs on the Pixel's CPU, and what it costs in power.

## Setup

- llama.cpp v0.5.0, cross-compiled on the laptop with clang against the
  phone's sysroot (`~/.cache/omarchy-webengine/sysroot`, the WebEngine
  build's): `-DGGML_NATIVE=OFF -DGGML_CPU_ARM_ARCH=armv8.2-a+dotprod+fp16
  -DBUILD_SHARED_LIBS=OFF -DGGML_OPENMP=OFF`, targets llama-cli,
  llama-bench, llama-server, installed in `/root/llm/bin`. Compiling it on
  the phone (4 jobs at full clocks plus a download) is what browned the
  phone out at 01:08 (`kernel/bcl`).
- Qwen2.5 0.5B and 1.5B Instruct, Q4_K_M GGUF (469 MB and 1.07 GB), checked
  against Hugging Face's SHA-256, in `/root/llm`.
- `scripts/pixel-llm-bench.sh`: llama-bench, 3 repetitions, pinned with
  `taskset` to the little cores (0-3), the two big cores (6-7) or big and
  mid (4-7); CPUs 4-7 held online; ODPM energy over each run; the battery
  voltage's low-water mark every 50 ms. Kernel v39 with brownout protection,
  on USB.

## Results

Idle during the test: 870 mW (screen on). `pp128` is prompt processing (128
tokens), `tg64` generation (64 tokens).

| Model | Cores | Prompt (tok/s) | Generation (tok/s) | Generation power over idle | Energy per generated token |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5B | little ×4 | 23.6 | 13.5 | 1.5 W | 111 mJ |
| 0.5B | big ×2 | 72.9 | **31.8** | 3.1 W | **99 mJ** |
| 0.5B | big+mid ×4 | **117.5** | 28.9 | 3.9 W | 135 mJ |
| 1.5B | little ×4 | 18.4 | 6.4 | 2.5 W | 396 mJ |
| 1.5B | big ×2 | 61.3 | **13.0** | 3.4 W | **260 mJ** |
| 1.5B | big+mid ×4 | **90.8** | 13.6 | 3.8 W | 278 mJ |

- Generation is limited by memory bandwidth: the mid cores add power, not
  speed. Two big cores are the most efficient for generation; big and mid
  together are fastest for the prompt.
- The battery never went below 4.27 V and the CPU sensors peaked at 69 C,
  under the 75 C cap.
- The little cores were not more efficient per token for the 1.5B model
  (they run longer, and the rest of the SoC stays awake meanwhile).

## What it means for the assistant

A command such as "turn on the torch": a 500-token prompt (instructions and
the tool list) and a 40-token answer. With the 1.5B model: about 5.5 s on big
and mid for the prompt and 3 s on the big cores for the answer, about 30 J,
or 0.04 % of the 19 Wh battery. A hundred such requests a day would cost
about 4 %. The prompt is the larger share; llama.cpp's prompt cache (the
fixed instructions and tool list processed once and kept) removes most of it.

The 0.5B model answers about 2.5x faster for 40 % of the energy, but needs
to be checked for tool-calling accuracy. Next: a small tool-calling test set
from the helpers in the control plan, run against both models, and
llama-server on a local socket with a cached system prompt.
