# Sleep, charging and battery — September 27, 2026

Follows [calling and texting](calling-20260927.md). The phone now charges to
full on the stock profile, can hold at 80 %, and **sleeps (s2idle) whenever
the screen is dark**. Incoming calls and texts wake it. The screen has a
timeout, and a background alarm wakes the phone every 15 minutes by default.

## Results

- **Charging to full.** `guacamole_charger.ko` replaces the built-in guarded
  policy (500 mA, 4.20 V) with the stock profile: 4.39 V float and up to 3 A
  between 16 and 45 °C. From a wall charger (BC1.2 DCP, 2 A input) the battery
  took about 1.1 A, against 500 mA before. The old cap at 88–89 % was the
  4.20 V float.
- **80 % limit.** Settings → Battery. Charging holds at 80 % and resumes at
  75 %; hold and resume were both seen on the phone.
- **Sleep that survives.** A 60 s s2idle trial with the modem online: the
  modem stayed registered on LTE, the sensor DSP kept running, and Wi-Fi
  reconnected on its own after resume.
- **Calls and texts wake it.** A call placed 35 s into a sleep woke the phone,
  which rang and was answered. A text woke it and arrived at once.
- **Automatic sleep.** Once the screen is dark (power key or screen timeout),
  the phone sleeps 10 s later. The user set a 1-minute timeout, turned the
  always-on display off, and saw the phone go to sleep and wake on the power
  key.

## Charging

`kernel/power/guacamole-charger/guacamole_charger.c` is a standalone platform
driver with no match table. `adapter/power/charging-start.sh` (installed as
`guacamole-charging-start`, run from session-prepare when
`/root/power-bringup/charging-enabled` exists) binds it through
`driver_override` in place of `qcom-smbx-charger`, then restores the saved
limit. On any failure it gives the charger back to the built-in driver, and
the module's release path restores the guarded registers.

- The profile comes from the stock device tree (18857 `sm8150-mtp.dtsi`,
  oplus charging framework):
  - 16–45 °C: 4.39 V float, up to 3 A (FCC register `0x61`, 50 mA steps).
  - 5–16 °C and 0–5 °C: 2.1 A and 1.45 A.
  - 45–50 °C: 4.13 V float, 1.05 A.
  - From 50 °C: no charging.
  - Termination at 310 mA. Recharge 100 mV below the float.
- The input limit follows the charger type: 500 mA from a computer port,
  1.5 A from a charging port, 2 A from a wall charger. The driver reads
  `APSD_RESULT` and sets `USBIN_ICL_CFG`, plus the HC-mode bit in
  `USBIN_ICL_OPTIONS`. Warp (the MCU at 0x26) is not spoken to.
- It latches off on any of these, clearing on unplug, at most three times per
  boot:
  - 4.445 V at the battery;
  - the charger's battery over-voltage flag;
  - current 400 mA above the programmed limit for three samples;
  - a register that does not read back;
  - 10 hours of charging.
- `charge_control_end_threshold` (50–100) is the limit. `battery.py limit N`
  sets it, and `battery.py restore` puts it back at boot from
  `$XDG_STATE_HOME/omarchy-mobile/charge-limit.json`.
- Charging stops while the phone is asleep (`gchg_suspend`). The sleep policy
  does not sleep on the charger, so this does not come up yet.

## Why sleep crashed the modem

The first s2idle trial resumed, but the modem watchdog fired 0.96 s later
with `RFLM@qsf_hl_seq.c:118 Assertion (rflm_qlnk_ls_retry_cnt < 2)`. That is
the QLink assertion from the
[SIM bring-up](cellular-sim-20260926.md#1-the-qlink-assertion-was-the-aps-mss-vote):
QLink cannot restart after the modem sleeps while the AP holds `mss.lvl` at
its top corner. `mss_vote.ko` drops that vote to 0 once the modem runs.

System suspend undoes it. The MSS power domain is off, with only the modem's
virtual device attached. genpd's `resume_noirq` powers every domain back on
(`genpd_sync_power_on`), and `genpd_complete` then queues it off again. Both
call rpmhpd, and rpmhpd clamps to the top corner until `sync_state`, which
never runs here. So after every suspend the AP was voting MSS at corner 9
again, and the modem's next RF wakeup asserted.

`pm_test` showed where:

| `pm_test` | Covers | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `freezer` | tasks frozen only | modem fine (sensor DSP crash, below) |
| `devices` | device suspend, no noirq | modem fine, Wi-Fi reconnects |
| `platform` | through noirq | **phone froze** (no log survived) |

`mss_vote.c` now adds a device of its own to the MSS domain
(`dev_pm_domain_attach_by_name` on the modem node) and registers a genpd power
notifier. After each `GENPD_NOTIFY_ON` and `GENPD_NOTIFY_OFF` it sends the
chosen corner again, so the top corner lasts microseconds. The module's
`reasserted` parameter counts these. With it:

- `pm_test=platform` passed, with two reasserts (`domain on`, `domain off`).
- The 60 s real trial and every sleep since have kept the modem up.

The RPMh sleep/wake sets were never the problem. `rpmh_votes.ko` (rebuilt for
#194) showed the MSS entry with no sleep vote and CX/MX with equal sleep and
wake votes, so the flush at power collapse changes nothing.

## Why sleep crashed the sensor DSP

At every thaw the SLPI died:
`EX:sensor_process:0x1:frpc_dsp:0x6c`, 6 ms after "Restarting tasks". A
freeze alone did it (`pm_test=freezer`).

- hexagonrpcd's listener is always blocked in a FastRPC invoke.
- The freezer interrupts it. Upstream fastrpc returns `-ERESTARTSYS`, and the
  restarted syscall sends the call again as a new message.
- The DSP then sees two copies of its pending call and crashes.

`kernel/sensors/fastrpc-freezable-wait.patch` waits with
`TASK_INTERRUPTIBLE | TASK_FREEZABLE`, so the freezer freezes the caller in
place, and a completion that arrives meanwhile is kept for the thaw. After
it, the SLPI survives freezes and sleeps, and the light sensor reports
normally.

## Calls and texts wake the phone

The modem's QMI traffic comes in on the glink-smem interrupt, which is
`IRQF_NO_SUSPEND`: it runs in s2idle, but the kernel never arms such an
interrupt for wakeup. The smp2p-mpss wake IRQ is wake-capable but disabled
by default, and it signals crashes, not messages. The CPU cluster already
power-collapses thousands of times at runtime with this interrupt still
waking it, so delivery was never the issue, only turning it into a system
wakeup.

`kernel/radio/qrtr-smd-wake.patch` (`scripts/build_qrtr_wake.sh`) parses each
packet in the transport's receive callback. For a QMI indication from the
`wake_ports` on `wake_node`, it raises a hard wakeup
(`pm_wakeup_ws_event(..., 3000, true)`), holding the phone awake 3 s for
ModemManager.

- Server ports change between modem boots: 73/85 on one boot, 71/87 on the
  next. `guacamole-radio-start` looks them up with `qrtr-lookup` for voice
  (service 9) and messaging (5) and writes them when
  `cellular/wake-on-modem` exists.
- The first wake came from the voice service's all-call-status indication
  (`0x002e`).
- The module counts wakes in `wake_events`. `qrtr-smd-wake` is its wakeup
  source.
- DIAG and every other service stay unable to wake the phone. The modem's
  glink traffic runs about 11 interrupts per second while the AP is awake,
  most of it DIAG while that is on.

## The sleep policy

Portable, in `overlay/mobile`:

- `sleep.py` (`omarchy-mobile-sleep run`, kept alive by the shell) sleeps
  when all of these hold:
  - the screen is dark (every monitor DPMS off);
  - it has been dark for 10 s;
  - no call is in progress (`calls.json`, `audio.json`);
  - no app audio is playing (uncorked sink-inputs, the audio graph's own
    loopbacks aside);
  - nothing is in `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/omarchy-mobile-sleep-inhibit/`.
- After a wake it stays up for a while, depending on what woke it:
  - modem: 15 s;
  - network: 10 s;
  - alarm: 45 s, for Wi-Fi to reconnect;
  - power key: no hold, because the screen lights.

  Each sleep is logged to `~/.local/state/omarchy-mobile/sleep.log` with
  what woke it, how long it slept, and the charge used.
- The screen timeout is an `IdleMonitor` in `shell.qml` (prefs
  `screenTimeout`, default 60 s). It never runs during a call, respects idle
  inhibitors, and restarts when the CRT open finishes, so a screen lit
  without input times out too. It calls `omarchy-mobile-display idle
  [ambient]`, which acts only on a lit screen and goes to the always-on
  display when that is chosen.
- `power-button.py` no longer suspends; it switches the display.
  - A wake press reaches it as an ordinary press on a dark screen.
  - Press and release now wait their turn for the lock. After a wake both
    start at once, and the old non-blocking lock dropped the release, so the
    first press did nothing.
  - A release that finds no press yet waits 0.4 s for its pair.
- Settings:
  - Display: Screen timeout (15 s – 10 min, or never).
  - Battery: sleep on/off, the background check (5, 15, 30, 60 min or
    never), and the last sleep.
  - Choices open in `kit/ChoicePicker.qml`.
  - Prefs: `sleep`, `sleepCheck`, `screenTimeout` in `prefs.py`.

Device side, `adapter/suspend.py` (`/usr/local/sbin/guacamole-suspend`):

- `--keep-dark` leaves the display alone and prints `{"woke", "slept"}`.
- The reason comes from `/sys/power/pm_wakeup_irq`, which is cleared at every
  suspend and names the IRQ from `/proc/interrupts` (`pm8941_pwrkey`,
  `pm8xxx_rtc_alarm`, `ipa`). A wake with no IRQ where qrtr-smd's
  `wake_events` rose is a call or text.
- `--wake-after` now allows up to an hour.
- It still refuses on the charger.

With the always-on display on, the screen is never dark, so the phone does
not sleep while the clock shows. It does sleep once face-down or pocket
detection switches the panel off.

## Drain

To be measured from `sleep.log` over long unplugged sleeps (the gauge counts
whole mAh). Earlier figures: 131 mA awake with the screen off, 83 mA in an
s2idle with CX/MX still clamped by rpmhpd. Android is about 25 mA asleep.

## Other fixes on the way

- **Notifications were never posted.** `messages.py` and `phone.py` called
  `gdbus call ... Notify ... -1`, and gdbus read the `-1` expiry as an option,
  so every text and missed-call notification failed silently. The unread
  count in the status bar comes from the watcher's own output, which hid it.
  Both calls now put `--` before the arguments, with regression tests.
- **The CRT open after a wake played on a dark panel.** Bringing the display
  hardware back after s2idle takes longer than the 420 ms open.
  `CrtPower.qml` now asks for the panel and starts the open on the second
  frame drawn after it, or after 1.5 s. The compositor draws no frames for a
  dark output.
- **Reboots over Wi-Fi.** `phone-crash-reboot.sh` stages its outer-root steps
  over SSH when USB is down, and falls back to SysRq-b when `reboot -f`
  stalls. It stalled with a crashed modem and with a crashed SLPI; the root is
  read-only by then.

## Shell and Settings changes the same day

- **Launcher.** The app drawer is alphabetical (case-insensitive by name).
  `DesktopEntries` order changed from one start to the next.
- **Status bar.** The CPU and memory bars are gone, and so is the 3 s
  `/proc` polling that fed them. A chip button in the shade's header, beside
  clipboard, opens the same Performance page: CPU, memory, cores,
  temperatures and top processes.
- **Weather.**
  - The location search moved from the shade to Settings → Weather
    (`kit/WeatherPage.qml`). Tapping the place name in the shade opens it.
  - The shade's weather page has a 24-hour strip under the current
    conditions, swiped sideways. Each hour shows time, icon, temperature and
    rain chance when it is 10 % or more.
  - `weather.py` asks Open-Meteo for hourly `temperature_2m`, `weather_code`,
    `precipitation_probability` and `is_day`. The strip starts at the
    location's current hour (from `utc_offset_seconds`), so a cached forecast
    never shows a past hour as "Now". A cache without hours is refetched.
  - Clear and partly clear hours after dark get moon icons.
- **Pickers.** Screen timeout and the background check open
  `kit/ChoicePicker.qml`, a sheet in the font picker's style, instead of
  cycling on each tap.
- **Unread texts.** The one text that arrived before the notification fix was
  marked read (`omarchy-mobile-messages read`) to clear the status-bar count.

## Incidents

- The `pm_test=platform` run and the second real trial froze the phone
  before the MSS fix. Both needed a forced restart. pstore was empty after
  each, because the forced reset loses the ramoops region.
- USB did not come up after either forced restart, the known pattern. The
  host saw the disconnect and nothing after, while the phone's UDC asserted
  pull-up and detected SDP. That points at the data lines not reaching the
  controller. The stock tree names a USB switch (`microchip,usb-sw-2-gpio`,
  TLMM 59) and the Warp MCU enable (TLMM 10). Both read as inputs, low and
  pulled down, unclaimed by Linux. Compare on a boot where USB works.
